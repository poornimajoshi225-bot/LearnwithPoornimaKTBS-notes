Class 10 English First Language Part 1

Poem 5

A Poison Tree

(For Memorisation)
William Blake

Pre-Reading Activity

All of us get angry sometime or the other. Is it good or bad to express one's anger? Why? Why not? Discuss in pairs.

I was angry with my friend:
I told my wrath, my wrath did end.
I was angry with my foe:
I told it not, my wrath did grow. 4
And I water'd it in fears,
Night and morning with my tears;
And I sunn'd it with smiles,
And with soft deceitful wiles. 8
And it grew both day and night,
Till it bore an apple bright;
And my foe beheld it shine,
And he knew that it was mine. 12
And into my garden stole
When the night had veil'd the pole:
In the morning glad I see
My foe outstretch'd beneath the tree. 16

I. GLOSSARY

Word Meaning
anger wrath (archaic)
foe enemy
wiles tricks
beheld saw
veiled covered
outstretch'd lying dead
apple a possible allusion to the forbidden fruit in The Bible
sunn'd (here used as a verb) gave light and heat; nourished
pole the pole-star

II. COMPREHENSION QUESTIONS

A. Answer the following questions briefly.

1. What happened when the speaker expressed his anger?

Answer: When the speaker told his friend about his anger, his wrath ended immediately.

2. What happened when the speaker suppressed his anger?

Answer: His anger continued to grow because he did not express it.

3. How are the results different in the two instances?

Answer: Expressing anger removes bitterness, while suppressing it makes it stronger and more destructive.

4. Which word in the first stanza suggests a tree?

Answer: grow.

5. How did the speaker help his anger grow?

Answer: He watered it with fears and tears, and sunned it with smiles and deceitful wiles.

6. "Water'd it in fears" suggests

  • ❌ a. the speaker did not have the courage to express his anger
  • ✅ b. the speaker was afraid of the consequences
  • ❌ c. the speaker didn't want to displease his enemy

7. The word "tears" suggests

  • ✅ a. the speaker too suffered for suppressing his anger
  • ❌ b. the enemy suffered
  • ❌ c. the speaker had to go through a lot of anxiety

8. "Sunn'd it with smiles" suggests

  • ✅ a. the speaker cunningly hid his anger with bright smiles
  • ❌ b. the speaker pretended that he was not angry
  • ❌ c. the speaker let his anger grow secretly

9. Which line tells you that the speaker is a scheming and cunning person?

Answer: "And sunn'd it with smiles, And with soft deceitful wiles."

10. Which words in the second stanza refer to a growing tree?

Answer: Water'd, sunn'd and grew.

11. The poet, so far, has been talking about

  • ❌ a. his suppressed anger
  • ✅ c. both

12. What kind of tree and fruit grow when nurtured by fears, tears and deceit?

Answer: A poisonous tree with a poisonous fruit symbolising hatred and revenge.

13. What does "it" refer to in the third stanza?

Answer: "It" refers to the tree of anger or wrath.

14. "Apple" in the poem refers to

  • ❌ a. the forbidden apple
  • ❌ b. the consequence of suppressing anger
  • ✅ c. the fruit of his enmity

15. The apple is "bright" because

  • ❌ a. it attracts the foe
  • ✅ b. it tempts the foe
  • ❌ c. it hides bitterness

16. Who stole into the speaker's garden?

Answer: The speaker's foe.

17. The word "stole" means

  • ❌ a. stole the apple
  • ✅ b. entered the garden secretly

18. What does the speaker see in the morning?

Answer: He sees his enemy lying dead beneath the tree.

19. Who dies at the end?

Answer: The enemy dies physically, while the speaker suffers a spiritual and emotional death because of his hatred.

20. "A Poison Tree" could refer to

  • ✅ the tree of hatred and enmity.
  • ✅ the destructive effects of hypocrisy and deceit.
  • ✅ the spiritual death caused by nurturing anger.

B. Close Study

Read the following extracts carefully. Discuss in pairs and then write the answers to the questions given below them.

Extract 1

“And into my garden stole
When the night had veiled the pole.”

a) Who stole into the garden?

Answer: The speaker's foe (enemy) stole into the garden.

b) Why did he steal into the garden?

Answer: He stole into the garden because he was tempted by the bright shining apple, unaware that it was a trap created by the speaker.

c) Explain the phrase "veiled the pole".

Answer: It means the darkness of the night covered the pole-star, creating a secretive and mysterious atmosphere.

Extract 2

"And I sunn'd it with smiles
And with soft deceitful wiles"

a) What does the word "sunn'd" suggest?

Answer: It suggests that the speaker nourished and encouraged his anger by hiding it behind fake smiles.

b) Explain the phrase "deceitful wiles".

Answer: It refers to dishonest tricks and cunning behaviour used to hide true feelings and mislead others.

c) Why had the speaker "sunn'd" it?

Answer: He hid his anger behind pleasant smiles so that it would continue to grow secretly and eventually destroy his enemy.

III. PARAGRAPH WRITING

Discuss in pairs or groups of four each, the answers to the following question. Note down the important points and then develop them into a paragraph.

1. How does the poet use the image of a tree to bring out the destructive effect of suppressed anger?

Answer:

In the poem "A Poison Tree", William Blake brilliantly employs the extended metaphor of a growing tree to illustrate how unexpressed anger festers and turns fatal. The imagery begins subtly in the first stanza when the speaker's unspoken wrath simply begins to "grow", but it rapidly unfolds into a full-fledged botanical metaphor in the second and third stanzas. The speaker actively cultivates this malicious plant by "watering" it with dark "fears" and bitter "tears", while simultaneously "sunning" it with fake "smiles" and "deceitful wiles." Just as a real sapling needs nourishment, sunlight and water to thrive, the speaker's hidden resentment is fed by negative emotions and hypocrisy, allowing it to flourish day and night. Eventually, this poisonous cultivation yields a bright and tempting "apple", symbolising the deadly fruit of hatred and revenge. When the speaker's foe secretly enters the garden and is tempted by the apple, he meets a tragic end beneath the tree. Through this powerful image, Blake teaches that expressing anger honestly helps resolve conflict, whereas suppressing it allows hatred to grow into a destructive force that harms both the victim and the person who nurtures it.
