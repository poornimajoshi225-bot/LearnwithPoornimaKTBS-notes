About the Lesson

"Karna" is adapted from the Mahabharata by C. Rajagopalachari. The lesson portrays Karna as one of the greatest warriors, admired for his bravery, generosity, loyalty and unwavering commitment to his promises. Despite knowing the truth about his birth, Karna remains loyal to Duryodhana, making him one of the most tragic heroes in Indian literature.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the character of Karna.
  • Know the values of loyalty and generosity.
  • Develop reading and comprehension skills.
  • Improve vocabulary.
  • Prepare for examinations.

Summary

Karna was born to Kunti through the blessings of the Sun God. He was abandoned at birth and raised by a charioteer's family. Though gifted with extraordinary skills, society looked down upon him because of his birth. Duryodhana recognized his talent and made him the King of Anga. Out of gratitude, Karna remained loyal to Duryodhana throughout his life. Even when Kunti revealed that he was her eldest son, he refused to abandon his friend. Karna's life teaches the values of generosity, courage, sacrifice and loyalty.

II. COMPREHENSION QUESTIONS

Answer briefly the following questions:

1. Why was a large crowd assembled in the arena?

Answer: A large crowd had assembled because a day had been fixed for a public test and exhibition of the Pandavas' and Kauravas' proficiency in the use of arms in the presence of the Royal family.

2. What did Duryodhana envy?

Answer: Duryodhana envied Arjuna's superhuman skill with his weapons, which left the vast assemblage lost in wonder and admiration.

3. How does the writer introduce Karna to the assembly?

Answer: The writer introduces Karna as a godlike youth who entered through the crowd while everyone watched in silence. Light and power seemed to emanate from him as he confidently approached Arjuna.

4. What was the cause for Duryodhana's exultation?

Answer: Duryodhana was delighted because Karna repeated all of Arjuna's feats with great ease and challenged him to a duel.

5. How does Karna challenge Arjuna?

Answer: Karna boldly challenged Arjuna by declaring that he would display even greater skill than Arjuna and then successfully repeated all his feats.

6. Who were the divine parents of the two heroes?

Answer: Arjuna was the son of Indra, while Karna was the son of Surya (the Sun God).

7. Why was Kunti stupefied?

Answer: Kunti was shocked because she immediately recognized Karna as her first-born son and was overcome with anguish.

8. Why did Kripacharya object to Karna joining in the battle?

Answer: Kripacharya objected because Karna's parentage and royal lineage were unknown, whereas only princes were allowed to engage in such combat.

9. How did Duryodhana restore Karna's honour?

Answer: Duryodhana immediately crowned Karna as the King of Anga, thereby giving him royal status and restoring his honour.

10. How does Bhima ridicule Karna?

Answer: Bhima mocked Karna by calling him the son of a charioteer and saying that he should hold the whip instead of fighting with princes.

11. How does Duryodhana defend Karna against Bhima's accusations?

Answer: Duryodhana defended Karna by saying that bravery, skill and character are more important than birth. He praised Karna's noble qualities and heroic appearance.

12. How and why did Indra approach Karna?

Answer: Indra came disguised as a Brahmin to ask for Karna's divine armour and earrings so that his son Arjuna would be protected in the coming war.

13(a). Why couldn't Karna refuse the gift asked of him?

Answer: Karna had taken a vow never to refuse anyone who came seeking charity. Therefore, he willingly gave away his divine armour and earrings.

13(b). What does this tell us about Karna's character?

Answer: It shows that Karna was generous, noble, truthful and willing to sacrifice even his own safety to keep his promise.

14. Why does Indra offer a boon to Karna? What is the boon?

Answer: Indra was deeply moved by Karna's unmatched generosity after receiving his divine armour and earrings. As a reward, he granted Karna the powerful weapon Sakti, which could kill even the mightiest enemy.

15. Indra granted the boon with a condition. What was the condition?

Answer: Indra said that the Sakti weapon could be used only once. After killing one enemy, it would immediately return to Indra and could never be used again.

16. How did Parasurama realise that Karna was not a Brahmana?

Answer: While Parasurama was sleeping on Karna's lap, a worm bored into Karna's thigh. Karna silently endured the unbearable pain without moving. Seeing such extraordinary endurance, Parasurama realized that only a Kshatriya could bear such suffering and concluded that Karna had deceived him.

17. What was Parasurama's curse?

Answer: Parasurama cursed Karna that the knowledge of the Brahmastra would fail him at the most critical moment in battle when he needed it the most.

18. What does Bhishma ask Karna to do?

Answer: Bhishma advised Karna to make peace with the Pandavas because they were his own brothers. However, he finally blessed Karna to remain loyal to Duryodhana and fight bravely.

19. What does Karna say about his loyalty to Duryodhana?

Answer: Karna said that Duryodhana had trusted and honoured him when everyone else insulted him. Therefore, he would never betray his friend and would remain loyal to him even at the cost of his own life.

20. How does Lord Krishna save Arjuna from Karna's arrow?

Answer: Lord Krishna suddenly pressed Arjuna's chariot deep into the ground. As a result, Karna's deadly arrow missed Arjuna's head and only knocked off his crown.

21. What mishap happened to Karna's chariot?

Answer: During the fierce battle, one wheel of Karna's chariot got stuck in the muddy battlefield, making it impossible for him to continue fighting properly.

22. Mention any three unfair acts of Karna and the Kauravas recalled by Lord Krishna.

Answer:

  • They insulted and humiliated Draupadi in the royal court.
  • They cheated Yudhishthira in the game of dice.
  • They surrounded and unfairly killed Abhimanyu in battle.

23(a). What was Karna's response to Lord Krishna's denouncement?

Answer: Karna bowed his head in shame and remained completely silent.

23(b). What does this tell you about Karna?

Answer: It shows that Karna was honest and had a clear conscience. He accepted the truth instead of trying to justify his mistakes.

24. Why was Arjuna's mind wavering when Lord Krishna asked him to slay Karna?

Answer: Arjuna hesitated because Karna was unarmed and was trying to lift the wheel of his chariot. According to the rules of war, attacking such a warrior was considered unfair.

25. Was killing Karna when he was not ready for the fight wrong?

Answer: According to the traditional rules of warfare, it was wrong to attack an unarmed warrior. Therefore, Arjuna initially hesitated.

26. How does the poet justify this wrong?

Answer: The poet explains that Lord Krishna reminded Arjuna of all the unjust and cruel actions committed by Karna and the Kauravas. Krishna instructed Arjuna to kill Karna to uphold justice and destroy evil.

III. DISCUSSION QUESTIONS

Discuss in pairs and answer the following questions.

1. Karna is one of the most tragic and compelling characters in the Mahabharata. List any five great qualities of Karna which you admire most. Justify your choice.

Answer:

  1. Generosity: Karna was famous for his charity. He never refused anyone who sought help, even when it meant sacrificing his own divine armour and earrings.
  2. Loyalty: He remained faithful to Duryodhana throughout his life because Duryodhana supported him when others insulted him.
  3. Courage: Karna fought fearlessly against great warriors despite knowing the dangers that awaited him.
  4. Determination: He worked hard to become a great warrior and never allowed difficulties or discrimination to stop him.
  5. Humility: When Lord Krishna reminded him of his mistakes, Karna remained silent and accepted the truth with dignity.

2. Epics highlight eternal values. List any five values from this lesson that are relevant to our lives today.

Answer:

  • Gratitude – Always be thankful to those who help us.
  • Loyalty – Stand by true friends during difficult times.
  • Generosity – Help others without expecting anything in return.
  • Courage – Face difficulties with confidence and determination.
  • Justice and Fairness – Always follow truth and uphold righteousness in life.

The life of Karna teaches us that qualities such as honesty, sacrifice, friendship, kindness and courage are timeless values that inspire people even today.
